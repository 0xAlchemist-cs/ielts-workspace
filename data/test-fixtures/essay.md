# Ocean Plastics: The Invisible Problem

Every year, roughly eight million tonnes of plastic enter the ocean. Currents gather this debris into vast floating patches, and sunlight breaks larger items into fragments small enough to enter the food chain. Scientists now find microplastics in fish, sea salt, and even drinking water.

The economics are stubborn. Plastic remains cheap to produce and expensive to collect, so voluntary cleanup efforts cannot keep pace with production. Several governments have therefore taxed single-use items or required deposits on bottles, and early evidence suggests that deposit schemes dramatically raise recycling rates.

Consumers hold real power as well. Choosing refillable containers, refusing plastic cutlery, and supporting brands that design for reuse all reduce demand at the source, which is ultimately cheaper than fishing the material back out of the sea.
